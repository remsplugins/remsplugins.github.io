How it works

1

Spin

Pull the lever or hit SPIN and all three reels roll. The RANDOM buttons reroll a single reel, the ‹ › arrows let you pick by hand, and a padlock keeps a reel you like while the others spin.

2

Listen

▶ plays the loop, 4 to 16 bars long. Set the tempo or click DAW SYNC to follow your project, roll the dice in CHORDS for another progression, and keep spinning until it clicks.

3

Cash out

Drag EXPORT TO DAW onto a track and the MIDI is there. Click it for MIDI and WAV tags you can drag too, or click a tag to save the file in Documents/Lucky Beats.

The house always helps you win

Chords that fit, dealt at random

The CHORDS panel shows the chords of the loop with their degrees, for example Am7, G7, Fmaj7, Em7 (i, VII, VI, v), and the chord that's playing lights up.

  • 12 progressions per mode, always in the key on the Tonality reel.
  • The dice deals a new progression, ‹ › browse them, and every SPIN draws a fresh one.
  • 4, 8, 12 or 16 bars, with a real song progression over the whole length (A B, A B C, A B A C). Tabs above the chords show each section.

Play it with your own synth

Click RANDOM GENERATOR VST on the left of the machine and pick any VST3 or AU instrument installed on your computer: Serum, Analog Lab, Kontakt, or anything else.

  • The reel picks the part (bass, chords, lead…) and your synth plays it.
  • Every spin loads one of its presets at random, and its name shows under the reel.
  • Preset picker: tick the presets the spins may draw from, with All, None and search. Click one to hear it.
  • My Sounds: for synths that keep their presets to themselves, open the synth's window, find a sound you love and click + Add to my sounds. The spins then draw from your list.
  • The WAV export is rendered with that sound.

From the reels to your track

  • Drag and drop: drag EXPORT TO DAW onto a track for the MIDI, or click it for MIDI and WAV tags that drag too. Works in Ableton Live and any DAW that accepts drag and drop.
  • DAW SYNC: the loop follows your project's tempo and bars.
  • Slot machine sounds for the lever, the reels and the jackpot bell. Turn them off with a right-click. They never end up in your exports.
  • A machine, not a box: on macOS the DAW's title bar is hidden, so only the machine shows. Move it by its body, close it with the big ×.
  • Standalone app: no DAW needed. The window takes the shape of the machine.
  • Resizable: right-click to change the size of the machine.

Install

macOS

  1. Unzip the download.
  2. Double-click fix_mac_plugins.command: it installs the VST3 and the AU, unblocks them and forces a rescan.
  3. Or copy VST3/LuckyBeats.vst3 to ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3 and AU/LuckyBeats.component to ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components. Logic and GarageBand only use the AU.
  4. Restart your DAW and rescan plug-ins. Lucky Beats is an instrument, by Rems Plugins.

Windows

  1. Unzip the download.
  2. Copy the LuckyBeats.vst3 folder (inside VST3) to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3.
  3. Restart your DAW and rescan plug-ins. In Ableton Live: Options → Preferences → Plug-Ins → Rescan.

If SmartScreen warns about the standalone app, click More info → Run anyway. It isn't code-signed yet.

Linux

  1. Unzip the download.
  2. Copy LuckyBeats.vst3 to ~/.vst3 and LuckyBeats.lv2 to ~/.lv2.
  3. Restart your DAW and rescan. Works in Bitwig, Reaper, Renoise, Tracktion, Ardour, Carla and Zrythm (x86_64, Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, Fedora 36 or newer).

If macOS blocks it

The plug-in is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS may block it. Paste this into Terminal, then restart your DAW:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/LuckyBeats.vst3 ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/LuckyBeats.component
